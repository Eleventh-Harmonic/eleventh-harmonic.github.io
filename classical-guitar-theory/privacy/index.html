Classical Guitar Theory Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 3, 2026

This policy explains how the Classical Guitar Theory app for Android handles information. Classical Guitar Theory is published by Eleventh Harmonic.

Privacy at a glance

  • The app sends nothing to us. It has no accounts, ads or tracking, and it does not use the microphone.
  • Your progress and settings are stored on your device, and in your Android backup if you use one.
  • Google Play handles purchases. We do not receive your payment card or bank account details.
  • Google’s billing library, which the app needs for purchases, sends Google technical diagnostics.
  • Separate test builds, given only to invited testers, send us usage, error and bug reports.
  • If you email us, we use your message to answer you and to improve the app.

Google is responsible for its own processing, described in the sections on purchases, diagnostics, crash reports and backups.

What the app does not do

The app you install from Google Play does not send information to us. It has no accounts or sign-in and does not use the advertising ID. It does not ask for permission to access your location, contacts, microphone, camera, photos or files.

We have not included any advertising, analytics, crash reporting or tracking software in the app. Google Play Billing Library, which the app needs for purchases, sends Google its own diagnostics, described in Diagnostics sent by Google Play Billing Library. Apart from that library, the app does not connect to the internet itself.

Test builds for invited testers are different: they send us reports, as described in Test builds for invited testers.

Information stored on your device

To teach you and to remember your choices, the app stores on your device:

  • your practice progress: which lessons you have played, your scores and your best times;
  • your settings, for example the app’s language, how notes are named, the practice reminder time and your Custom Reading choices;
  • whether the full course is unlocked;
  • a record of which exercises you opened and how each one ended;
  • a random number created when the app was installed.

The app does not send this information to us. Android keeps it in the app’s private storage, separate from other apps.

If backup is turned on for your device, Android may include this information in your device backup and restore it on a new device. Google stores these backups in your Google Account under its own privacy policy. We do not have access to your backups.

Purchases through Google Play

The app offers one optional in-app purchase: an unlock for the rest of the course. Google Play handles the purchase and the payment under the Google Play Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy. Google collects your payment details directly. The app does not receive them, and Google does not give them to us.

The app uses Google Play Billing Library, made by Google, to talk to Google Play. When the app starts, it asks Google Play for the price of the unlock and whether your Google Account already owns it, for example after you reinstall the app. When you choose to buy, it starts the purchase and then confirms the completed purchase to Google Play.

The app also asks Google Play for the country of your Google Play account, as a two-letter code such as NL. The app you install from Google Play keeps this code in memory only and does not store or send it. It is not your location.

Google shares limited order information with us in the Google Play Console, for example the order number, product, price, date, status and the buyer’s country or region. Google also lets us find an order by the buyer’s email address, for example when you ask us for help with a purchase. We use this information to manage orders and refunds, to help you with purchases, and to meet our tax and accounting obligations. Our legal bases under the GDPR are our legitimate interest in managing purchases and helping you (Article 6(1)(f)) and our legal obligations (Article 6(1)(c)).

Diagnostics sent by Google Play Billing Library

When the app uses Google Play Billing Library, including when the app starts, the library may send Google technical information about its own operation, such as whether billing requests succeed or fail and problems with the connection to Google Play. Google’s software adds basic device and app details to this information: Android version, device model and manufacturer, system build, language and country setting, time zone, network type, mobile network operator code and the app’s version number.

This information goes directly to Google over an encrypted connection, not to us. Google says it uses it to improve the library and to provide better support for errors. Google decides how it uses this information, under the Google Privacy Policy.

Crash reports from Google Play

If you allow your device to share usage and diagnostics data with Google, Google Play may send us reports when the app crashes or stops responding. These reports contain technical details, such as the device model, the Android version and where in the app the problem happened. Google collects them under its own privacy policy and shows them to us in the Google Play Console. We use them only to find and fix problems in the app.

Android permissions

  • Notifications: lets the app show the practice reminder, if you turn it on.
  • Run at startup: sets the practice reminder again after your device restarts.
  • Vibration: added by the notification library, so that a reminder can vibrate.
  • Google Play billing: needed for the in-app purchase.
  • Internet and network state: added by Google Play Billing Library so it can talk to Google Play and send its diagnostics to Google.
  • Bluetooth, and location on Android 11 and older: added by a library for MIDI instruments. The app includes it because it shares its code with another of our apps. This app has no MIDI feature. It never asks for these permissions and never uses them, and it never reads your location.

The practice reminder

The app can remind you to practise once a day. The reminder is off until you turn it on, and you choose the time. Your device creates and shows the reminder itself. Nothing is sent to us or to a server to produce it, and the app keeps no record of whether you opened it.

Rating the app

Rate this app in Settings opens the Google Play Store app, and only when you tap it. The app does not see what you write there. Google Play’s own terms and privacy policy apply to what you do in it.

Test builds for invited testers

Before a release, we give a separate test build of the app to a small group of invited testers. This section does not apply to the app you install from Google Play for everyone. The test build sends us three kinds of reports.

Every report includes the random number created when the app was installed. It is not linked to any account, name, email address or advertising ID. It shows that several reports came from the same installation, but not who sent them. Uninstalling the app removes it from the device, but a device backup can restore it if the app is installed again.

Bug reports a tester sends

The test build shows a button for reporting a problem. When the tester taps it, and only then, the app takes a screenshot and shows it with a box for a comment and a button to record a spoken memo. It lists what will be attached, and nothing is sent unless the tester taps Send. The test build asks for the microphone only to record this memo.

A sent report contains the comment or the memo, the screenshot, the app version, the device brand, maker and model, whether the device is an emulator, the Android version, the screen size, which screen of the app was open, and the app’s language, note-naming system and grade at that moment.

How the test build is used

The test build sends a record of which exercises were opened, how long they lasted and how they ended, and how many questions were answered and how many were right. The record contains no free text: every value is a fixed name the app defines, a count, or a yes or no. It never records what a tester wrote, played or answered, only how many answers were right.

Each record also contains the app version, the device brand and model, the Android version, the country of the Google Play account (a two-letter code such as NL) and the app’s language (a two-letter code such as en). The country comes from Google Play, not from the device’s location. The app discards any answer that is not a two-letter country code, so nothing more precise than a country can be recorded.

Automatic error reports

When something goes wrong inside the app, the test build records the error and sends it the next time the app opens or closes normally. An error report contains the error message and the technical stack trace, how many times it happened, which screen it happened on, the app version, the device brand and model and the Android version. The app’s own code writes the error message, and the app shortens it before sending.

How we handle test reports

The reports go over an encrypted connection to a database that Supabase, Inc. hosts for us. We use them only to find and fix problems in the app and to see whether its teaching works. Our legal basis under the GDPR is our legitimate interest in finding and fixing problems before a release (Article 6(1)(f)). We keep the reports only as long as we need them for that, and we delete a tester’s reports when they ask.

Emails you send us

If you email us, we receive your email address, your name if your email account shows it, your message and anything you include, such as screenshots. Emailing us is optional. You do not need to give us any personal information to use the app.

We use this information to answer you, to help you with a purchase, and to fix problems and improve the app. Our legal basis under the GDPR is our legitimate interest in answering you and improving the app (Article 6(1)(f)).

We receive email with Gmail, a Google service. Google stores the messages on its servers, which may be in other countries, including the United States. Google says it relies on the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework and on standard contractual clauses for such transfers; see Google’s data transfer frameworks.

Who receives information

We do not sell or rent personal information, and we do not use or share it for advertising. We do not use it for automated decision-making or profiling.

  • Google receives information as the provider of Google Play (purchases, order information and crash reports), Google Play Billing Library (diagnostics) and Gmail (emails you send us). For Google Play and the billing library, Google decides how it uses the information, under its own terms and privacy policy.
  • Supabase, Inc. hosts the database that receives reports from test builds, and processes them only to provide that service.
  • Authorities or others may receive information if the law requires us to disclose it.

We do not give your information to anyone else.

Security

Android keeps the information the app stores private to the app. Google Play Billing Library sends its diagnostics over an encrypted connection, and test builds send their reports over an encrypted connection. We take reasonable steps to protect the emails and reports we receive, but no method of storage or transmission is completely secure.

Retention and deletion

  • Progress and settings on your device stay until you clear the app’s storage or uninstall the app. Uninstalling removes them from your device. A copy in your device backup follows your Google Account backup settings.
  • Your purchase is linked to your Google Account, so you can restore it after you reinstall the app. Uninstalling the app does not remove it. Google keeps purchase records under its own policies.
  • Order information stays in the Google Play Console under Google’s retention rules. If we keep copies for tax or accounting, we keep them only as long as those laws require.
  • Billing Library diagnostics and crash reports are kept by Google under its own policies.
  • Test build reports are kept only as long as we need them to find and fix problems, and are deleted when the tester asks.
  • Emails are kept as long as we need them to answer you and to follow up on the problem you reported. We delete them when you ask, unless the law requires us to keep them.

To delete the app’s data from your device, uninstall the app, or open your device settings, go to Apps, then Classical Guitar, then Storage, and clear the storage. To have your emails or test reports deleted, write to us.

Children

Classical Guitar Theory is intended for users aged 13 and older and is not directed at children under 13. We do not collect personal information through the app from anyone, including children. Google processes information for purchases, billing diagnostics and crash reports as described above. If you believe a child under 13 has sent us personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

Your rights

Under the GDPR and similar laws, you have the right to:

  • access the personal information we hold about you and get a copy of it;
  • have it corrected or deleted;
  • restrict or object to its use;
  • receive it in a portable format.

The app you install from Google Play does not collect or transmit personal information to us. If you contact us by email, we receive the information you choose to share. If you purchase the unlock, we can access limited order information through Google Play Console. If you are a tester, we hold the reports your test build sent; because they are linked only to a random number, tell us the date and device of a report so that we can find them. We do not maintain our own customer database. To use your rights, email us. We normally answer within one month.

You can also complain to a data protection authority: in the Netherlands, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, or the authority in the country where you live or work.

Google is responsible for the information it processes for Google Play, purchases, backups, crash reports and billing diagnostics. You can manage much of it in your Google Account.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when the app, the services it uses or the law changes. We will post every update on this page and change the “Last updated” date. If a change is significant, we will also mention it in the app’s release notes.
