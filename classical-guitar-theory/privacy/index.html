Classical Guitar Theory Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 22, 2026

This policy explains how the Classical Guitar Theory app for Android handles information. Classical Guitar Theory is published by Eleventh Harmonic.

Privacy at a glance

  • The app sends us nothing unless you are 16 or older and choose to share practice data. It has no accounts, ads or tracking, and it does not use the microphone.
  • Your progress and settings are stored on your device. Your progress can also be in your Android backup; your settings and your year of birth are never backed up.
  • Google Play handles purchases. We do not receive your payment card or bank account details.
  • Google’s billing library, which the app needs for purchases, sends Google technical diagnostics.
  • Separate test builds, given only to invited adult testers, send us usage, error and bug reports.
  • If you email us, we use your message to answer you and to improve the app.

What the app does not do

The app you install from Google Play sends us nothing unless you are 16 or older and choose to share practice data (see Practice data you choose to share). It has no accounts or sign-in and does not use the advertising ID. It does not ask for permission to access your location, contacts, microphone, camera, photos or files.

We have not included any third-party advertising, analytics, crash reporting or tracking software in the app. Google Play Billing Library, which the app needs for purchases, sends Google its own diagnostics, described in Diagnostics sent by Google Play Billing Library. Apart from that library, the app connects to the internet only to send practice data you chose to share.

Test builds for invited testers are different: they send us reports, as described in Test builds for invited testers.

Information stored on your device

To teach you and to remember your choices, the app stores on your device:

  • your practice progress: which lessons you have played, your scores and your best times;
  • your settings, for example the app’s language, how notes are named, the practice reminder time and your Custom Reading choices;
  • whether the full course is unlocked;
  • a record of which exercises you opened and how each one ended;
  • a record of errors that happened inside the app;
  • your year of birth, and whether you chose to share practice data;
  • a random number created when the app was installed, which Settings shows as your Installation number.

The app does not send this information to us, except the practice data you choose to share. Your year of birth never leaves your device. Android keeps it all in the app’s private storage, separate from other apps.

If backup is turned on for your device, Android may include your progress, whether the full course is unlocked, the records of exercises and errors, and the random installation number in your device backup, and restore them on a new device. Your settings, your year of birth and your choice about sharing practice data are never included in a backup or in a transfer to a new device. Google stores these backups in your Google Account under its own privacy policy. We do not have access to your backups.

Purchases through Google Play

The app offers one optional in-app purchase: an unlock for the rest of the course. Google Play handles the purchase and the payment under the Google Play Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy. Google collects your payment details directly. The app does not receive them, and Google does not give them to us.

The app uses Google Play Billing Library, made by Google, to talk to Google Play. When the app starts, it asks Google Play for the price of the unlock and whether your Google Account already owns it, for example after you reinstall the app. When you choose to buy, it starts the purchase and then confirms the completed purchase to Google Play.

The app also asks Google Play for the country of your Google Play account, as a two-letter code such as NL. It is not your location. The app keeps this code in memory only and never stores it. It sends the code to us only together with practice data you choose to share (see Practice data you choose to share).

Google shares limited order information with us in the Google Play Console, for example the order number, product, price, date, status and the buyer’s country or region. Google also lets us find an order by the buyer’s email address, for example when you ask us for help with a purchase. We use this information to manage orders and refunds, to help you with purchases, and to meet our tax and accounting obligations. Our legal bases under the GDPR are our legitimate interest in managing purchases and helping you (Article 6(1)(f)) and our legal obligations (Article 6(1)(c)).

Practice data you choose to share

When you first open the app, it asks your year of birth. The year stays on your device and is never sent. Only if you are certainly 16 or older (the app counts from the year alone, so it waits until the year after your 16th birthday year) does the app then ask whether you want to share practice data. The question lists what would be sent and links to this policy. Nothing is shared unless you choose Yes. You can turn it on or off at any time in Settings, under Share practice data.

If you choose Yes, the app sends us:

  • a record of which exercises you open, when, how long they last and how they end, and how many questions you answered and how many were right. It contains no free text: every value is a fixed name the app defines, a count, or a yes or no. It never records what you wrote, played or answered, only how many answers were right;
  • reports of errors inside the app: the error message (written by the app or by the software libraries it uses, shortened to 500 characters), the technical stack trace (shortened to 4,000 characters), when and how often it happened, and on which screen;
  • with each record: the random number created when the app was installed, the app version and build number, the device brand and model, and the Android version. Records of exercises also contain the country of your Google Play account (a two-letter code such as NL, not your location) and the app’s language.

Nothing recorded before you chose Yes is sent, and nothing is sent after you turn it off. The app has no button for sending screenshots, recordings or messages; that exists only in test builds.

We use this data only to find problems in the app and to see which lessons need work. Our legal basis under the GDPR is your consent (Article 6(1)(a)). You can withdraw it at any time in Settings; that stops further sending but does not undo what was already sent, which we delete when you ask. The data goes over an encrypted connection to a database that Supabase, Inc. hosts for us as a service provider, in the European Union (Ireland). The records we store do not contain your IP address. A daily cleanup automatically deletes every record older than 180 days, counted from when we received it. We delete records sooner when you ask: email us the installation number from the app’s Settings, and we delete every record and error report sent with it. Like any internet service, Supabase’s servers see the IP address of your device when the app connects. Supabase may keep it in its own service logs, and may keep backups of the database, for limited periods that Supabase sets; those copies are not part of the 180 days.

Diagnostics sent by Google Play Billing Library

When the app uses Google Play Billing Library, the library may send Google technical information about its own operation, such as whether billing requests succeed or fail and problems with the connection to Google Play. The app uses the library every time it starts, to check the price of the unlock and whether your Google Account already owns it, and again when you buy or restore the unlock. This happens for every user, at any age, and before the app asks your year of birth, because the app needs it to know whether the full course is unlocked. The app has no setting that turns it off.

Google’s software adds basic device and app details to this information: the Android version, the device model and manufacturer, the device’s hardware and product names, the system build, the language and country setting, the time zone, the network type, the mobile network operator code and the app’s version number. The library may keep this information on your device for a short time and send it later, also when the app is not open.

This information goes directly to Google over an encrypted connection, not to us, and we cannot see it. Google says it uses it to improve the performance of the library and to provide better support for errors. Google has not published a complete list of what the library sends, so the details above are what we could check in the library ourselves, not a statement from Google. We found no sign that it sends the advertising ID, the phone number, or hardware identifiers such as the IMEI or the serial number. The library does not ask for permission to access your location. Google decides how it uses this information, under the Google Privacy Policy.

Crash reports from Google Play

If you allow your device to share usage and diagnostics data with Google, Google Play may send us reports when the app crashes or stops responding. These reports contain technical details, such as the device model, the Android version and where in the app the problem happened. Google collects them under its own privacy policy and shows them to us in the Google Play Console. We use them only to find and fix problems in the app.

Android permissions

  • Notifications: lets the app show the practice reminder, if you turn it on.
  • Run at startup: sets the practice reminder again after your device restarts.
  • Vibration: added by the notification library, so that a reminder can vibrate.
  • Google Play billing: needed for the in-app purchase.
  • Internet and network state: added by Google Play Billing Library so it can talk to Google Play and send its diagnostics to Google. The app also uses the internet to send practice data you chose to share.

The practice reminder

The app can remind you to practise once a day. The reminder is off until you turn it on, and you choose the time. Your device creates and shows the reminder itself. Nothing is sent to us or to a server to produce it, and the app keeps no record of whether you opened it.

Rating the app

Rate this app in Settings opens the Google Play Store app, and only when you tap it. The app does not see what you write there. Google Play’s own terms and privacy policy apply to what you do in it.

Test builds for invited testers

Before a release, we give a separate test build of the app to a small group of invited adult testers. This section does not apply to the app you install from Google Play for everyone. The test build does not ask the year of birth or whether to share practice data. When remote diagnostics are enabled for the build, the usage and error reports described below may be sent. It sends a bug report only when the tester taps Send. A tester who does not want this can stop using the test build and ask us to delete their reports.

Every report includes the random number created when the app was installed, which the app’s Settings shows as the Installation number. It is not linked to any account, name, email address or advertising ID. It shows that several reports came from the same installation, but not who sent them. Uninstalling the app removes it from the device, but a device backup can restore it if the app is installed again.

Bug reports a tester sends

The test build shows a button for reporting a problem. When the tester taps it, and only then, the app takes a screenshot and shows it with a box for a comment and a button to record a spoken memo. It lists what will be attached, and nothing is sent unless the tester taps Send. The test build asks for the microphone only to record this memo.

A sent report contains the comment or the memo, the screenshot, the app version, the device brand, maker and model, whether the device is an emulator, the Android version, the screen size, which screen of the app was open, and the app’s language, note-naming system and grade at that moment.

How the test build is used

The test build sends a record of which exercises were opened, when, how long they lasted and how they ended, and how many questions were answered and how many were right. The record contains no free text: every value is a fixed name the app defines, a count, or a yes or no. It never records what a tester wrote, played or answered, only how many answers were right.

Each record also contains the app version, the device brand and model, the Android version, the country of the Google Play account (a two-letter code such as NL) and the app’s language (a two-letter code such as en). The country comes from Google Play, not from the device’s location. The app discards any answer that is not a two-letter country code, so nothing more precise than a country can be recorded.

Automatic error reports

When something goes wrong inside the app, the test build records the error and sends it the next time the app opens or closes normally. An error report contains the error message and the technical stack trace, how many times it happened, which screen it happened on, the app version, the device brand and model and the Android version. The error message can come from the app or from the software libraries it uses; the app shortens it to 500 characters, and the stack trace to 4,000 characters, before sending.

How we handle test reports

The reports go over an encrypted connection to a database that Supabase, Inc. hosts for us in the European Union (Ireland). We use them only to find and fix problems in the app and to see whether its teaching works. Our legal basis under the GDPR is our legitimate interest in finding and fixing problems before a release (Article 6(1)(f)). A daily cleanup automatically deletes usage and error reports older than 180 days, counted from when we received them. Bug reports are kept only as long as we need them to find and fix the problem. We delete a tester’s reports sooner when they ask.

Emails you send us

If you email us, we receive your email address, your name if your email account shows it, your message and anything you include, such as screenshots. Emailing us is optional. You do not need to give us any personal information to use the app.

We use this information to answer you, to help you with a purchase, and to fix problems and improve the app. Our legal basis under the GDPR is our legitimate interest in answering you and improving the app (Article 6(1)(f)).

We receive email with Gmail, a Google service. Google stores the messages on its servers, which may be in other countries, including the United States. Google says it relies on the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework and on standard contractual clauses for such transfers; see Google’s data transfer frameworks.

Who receives information

We do not sell or rent personal information, and we do not use or share it for advertising. We do not use it for automated decision-making or profiling.

  • Google receives information as the provider of Google Play (purchases, order information and crash reports), Google Play Billing Library (diagnostics) and Gmail (emails you send us). For Google Play and the billing library, Google decides how it uses the information, under its own terms and privacy policy.
  • Supabase, Inc. hosts, in the European Union (Ireland), the database that receives practice data you choose to share and reports from test builds, and processes them only to provide that service. Its servers may keep connection details, such as IP addresses, in service logs for a limited time.
  • Authorities or others may receive information if the law requires us to disclose it.

We do not give your information to anyone else.

Security

Android keeps the information the app stores private to the app. Google Play Billing Library sends its diagnostics over an encrypted connection, and practice data and test reports are sent over an encrypted connection. We take reasonable steps to protect the emails and reports we receive, but no method of storage or transmission is completely secure.

Retention and deletion

  • Progress and settings on your device stay until you clear the app’s storage or uninstall the app. Uninstalling removes them from your device. A copy of your progress in your device backup follows your Google Account backup settings; your settings and year of birth are never backed up.
  • Your purchase is linked to your Google Account, so you can restore it after you reinstall the app. Uninstalling the app does not remove it. Google keeps purchase records under its own policies.
  • Order information stays in the Google Play Console under Google’s retention rules. If we keep copies for tax or accounting, we keep them only as long as those laws require.
  • Billing Library diagnostics and crash reports are kept by Google under its own policies.
  • Practice data you shared is deleted by a daily cleanup once it is older than 180 days, counted from when we received it, or sooner when you ask. Supabase’s service logs and backups follow Supabase’s own retention periods.
  • Test build reports: usage and error reports are deleted by the same daily cleanup once they are older than 180 days; bug reports are kept only as long as we need them to find and fix problems. All of them are deleted sooner when the tester asks.
  • Emails are kept as long as we need them to answer you and to follow up on the problem you reported. We delete them when you ask, unless the law requires us to keep them.

To delete the app’s data from your device, uninstall the app, or open your device settings, go to Apps, then Classical Guitar, then Storage, and clear the storage. To have your emails, shared practice data or test reports deleted, write to us.

Children

Classical Guitar Theory is designed for learners aged 9 and older, children and adults alike. Google Play lists it for these ages and applies its Families policy to it.

We receive no personal information from children through the app you install from Google Play. It has no accounts, ads or tracking, and it does not ask for permission to access location, the microphone, the camera or contacts. Learners under 16 are never asked to share practice data, and the app sends nothing to us from them. The app relies on the year of birth the learner enters, and that year stays on their device. Children can use every lesson without sharing practice data. Google Play Billing Library sends Google the technical diagnostics described in Diagnostics sent by Google Play Billing Library from every device, including children’s. Google also processes information for purchases and crash reports as described above, and Google Play’s parental controls and purchase approvals apply to purchases.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. Test builds are given only to invited adult testers. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, for example by email, contact us and we will delete it.

Your rights

Under the GDPR and similar laws, you have the right to:

  • access the personal information we hold about you and get a copy of it;
  • have it corrected or deleted;
  • restrict or object to its use;
  • receive it in a portable format.

You can open this policy at any time from the app’s Settings. The app you install from Google Play transmits nothing to us unless you chose to share practice data. If you contact us by email, we receive the information you choose to share. If you purchase the unlock, we can access limited order information through Google Play Console. If you chose to share practice data, or you are a tester, we hold what your app sent. It is linked only to the installation number, which you find in the app’s Settings with a Copy button. Send us that number and we delete everything linked to it. Reinstalling the app or clearing its storage can make a new number; for data from an earlier installation, tell us the dates and the device model so that we can find it. You can stop sharing practice data at any time in Settings. We do not maintain our own customer database. To use your rights, email us. We normally answer within one month.

You can also complain to a data protection authority: in the Netherlands, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, or the authority in the country where you live or work.

Google is responsible for the information it processes for Google Play, purchases, backups, crash reports and billing diagnostics. You can manage much of it in your Google Account.
